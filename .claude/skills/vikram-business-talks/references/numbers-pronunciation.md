# Number & Financial-Figure Pronunciation (Tenglish)

Goal: Vikram reads numbers naturally and the viewer *feels* their size.
Status: **[DEFAULT]** conventions. Replace with Vikram's own habits once imported.

## 1. Always use the Indian system
| Figure | Say | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| 1,00,000 | oka laksha (1 lakh) | "one hundred thousand" |
| 10,00,000 | padi lakshalu (10 lakhs) | "one million" |
| 1,00,00,000 | oka koti (1 crore) | "ten million" |
| ₹2,500 crore | rendu vela aidu vandala kotlu (₹2,500 crore) | "25 billion rupees" |
| $1 billion | daadapu ₹8,300+ kotlu (state rate & date) | leaving it in dollars only |

English "lakh / crore" are fine in Tenglish ("2,500 crores") when that sounds more natural; pick
one style per video and stay consistent.

## 3. Rules
1. **Round for speech, exact on screen.** Say "daadapu 1,200 kotlu"; show "₹1,187 Cr".
2. **Max 2 numbers per sentence.**
3. **Make it relatable** with one of: per day ("roju ki…"), per person ("okko customer meeda…"),
   comparison ("mana Hyderabad metro project kanna ekkuva").
4. **Percentages:** "20 percent", then translate: "ante prathi 100 rupayallo 20 rupayalu".
5. **Profit vs revenue:** always say which. "Revenue ante ammakalu, profit ante kharchulu poyaka migiledi."
6. **Loss:** "nashtam". Negative growth: "taggindi", not "negative growth".
7. **Years:** "2019 lo" (say in English digits: "twenty nineteen") **[DEFAULT, confirm]**.
8. **Dates & periods:** say "FY24 ante March 2024 tho mugisina sanvatsaram" the first time.
9. **Currency conversions:** mention the rate once in the description, not in narration.
10. **Check after writing.** Every spoken figure must match the W1 facts table and the on-screen graphic.
